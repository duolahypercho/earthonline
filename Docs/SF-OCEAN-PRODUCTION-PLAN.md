# SF coast and Bay water production plan

Build one continuous place that a player can recognize from shore, enter, explore and resurface in. The scope is every playable water tile inside the current SF map, from the outer Pacific shelf through the Golden Gate into the mapped Bay. The first build should establish the shape and character of that whole area. Spend finish-quality effort early on three contrasting swims, while changes to the shared water and habitat rules are still inexpensive.

## Where we start

The native game already joins the coast and Bay. It has mapped seafloor elevation, regional water optics, swimming, Blender-built marine forms, fish, coastal plant communities and optional reef materials. Mile Rocks has the most developed reef study. Its repeated kelp silhouettes, even lighting and unfamiliar underwater wave motion are explicit review targets. Regional relief outside the detailed city grid is roughly 400 m in resolution. Individual rocks, plants and animals are habitat reconstructions. A detailed-looking ledge alone cannot establish the real depth or geology of a location. [Playable sites and current features](../native/README.md#swim-the-pacific-and-san-francisco-bay)

## The first map

Make a map of depth, bottom type, wave exposure, available light and source coverage before commissioning more individual assets. Overlay swimming routes, shore landmarks and boundaries where habitats meet. Record which features come from surveys, which are inferred from nearby conditions, and where the best available answer is a coarse regional fallback. Set every dataset against one sea-level reference. Use the existing elevation sources and cross-check offshore interpretation against published seafloor maps. [Existing data credits](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) · [USGS offshore San Francisco map series](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/california-state-waters-map-series-offshore-san-francisco-california)

| Area to cover | What a player should experience | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ocean Beach and open Pacific sand | A beach entry, breaking water, bars or troughs where supported, broad stretches of exposed sand and patches of coarse sediment. | Swim perpendicular to shore. Compare the depth profile to the source and watch where foam gives way to suspended sand. |
| Mile Rocks and exposed rocky coast | A readable ledge, broken rock at its foot, open sand between outcrops and patchy kelp rooted to lit rock. | Follow the bottom from canopy to open water. Check plant attachment, distant silhouettes and light beneath canopy gaps. |
| Golden Gate channel and headlands | A channel whose scale reads on descent, current around headlands and the bridge visible on suitable approaches. | Cross the channel edge, look up and resurface. Compare the depth and shoreline silhouette in both directions. |
| Aquatic Park and Black Point | Sheltered shallows beside beach and rock, a rocky intertidal margin and recognizable park trees and waterfront above. | Walk in, swim along the mixed bottom, then exit at the beach. Check wet surfaces, collision and waterline continuity. |
| Central Bay and the Ferry Building | Shorter underwater sight lines, soft bottom where supported, pilings under the waterfront and a recognizable skyline on surfacing. | Dive alongside a piling and look back up. Check scale, shadow, regional water color and alignment with the pier. |
| Mapped East and South Bay | Soft sediment, shallow banks, tidal flats and channels according to source coverage. Add eelgrass beds where habitat evidence supports them. | Cross a bank into a channel. Check shallow grades, plant boundaries and the transition to deeper water. |
| Outer shelf and map margins | Broad offshore relief, stretches of open water and sparse bottom detail appropriate to depth and available light. | Swim across the detailed-to-coarse data boundary. Check for steps, visible seams or sudden changes in color and animal density. |

These are regional art briefs. Use the survey wherever it resolves the shape. Depth should change convincingly along a swim, including places that become shallow again. Preserve bars, banks and shelves when the source shows them.

## What to build up front and what to refine later

| Workstream | Needed in the first playable pass | Detail that can follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seafloor and collision | Continuous shore, seabed and collision in the same coordinates. Define rock, sand, gravel and soft mud, with boundaries that follow slope, exposure and substrate evidence. | Sculpt local fractures, partly buried boulders and shell deposits within the accuracy of the underlying relief. |
| Surface and shore | Connect wind, swell, breakers and reflections to the existing weather. A cloud passing over the shore should soften the light on the seabed on the same swim. Establish a tide reference. Align cliffs, bridges, trees and piers with the playable shore. | Local foam streaks, spray, marine fog over the headlands and wetting patterns. Introduce changing tide height together with exposed terrain, shore access and collision. |
| Underwater light and motion | Set Pacific and Bay visibility ranges from regional references. Nearby rock should retain texture while distant features lose contrast with distance and depth. Keep the swimmer's view steady. Put motion on the surface overhead, suspended material and anchored plants. | Refine broken sunlight over shallow sand, canopy shadows, muted light under piers and sediment drifting in patches. Test clear sky, fog and low sun. |
| Bottom materials and vegetation | Establish rock families with distinct silhouettes, sand grain and ripple scale, matte rockweed, kelp with anchored bases and flexible blades, understory and sheltered grass beds. Define where each can grow, leave gaps, and preserve the silhouette from a distance. | Add chipped edges, different blade ages, shell burial and local growth on stone. Vary colonies as groups so the background avoids rows of identical plants. [NOAA kelp habitat reference](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/west-coast/habitat-conservation/kelp-forest-habitat-west-coast) |
| Wildlife | Check candidate species such as rockfish, schooling anchovies and bottom-dwelling flatfish against local records for each site. Establish distinct shapes, sizes, swimming behavior, response to the diver and limits by depth and habitat. | Add species, life stages, invertebrate clusters and occasional encounters after the common behaviors read clearly in motion. |
| Swimming, sound and scale | Verify walk-in, dive, ascent and exit, rock clearance and smooth changes between air and submerged sound. Use pilings, rocks, fish and surface light to convey size and direction. Record routes at player speed. | Refine local surf, harbor ambience, stroke sounds and subtle current cues after transitions work throughout the map. |
| Streaming and frame time | Price the worst visible combinations early, including overlapping kelp, city behind the waterline, shore reflections and a newly loaded tile. Set distance, animation and draw budgets from those measurements. | Spend spare time on close-up shapes and materials. Verify that distant simplification preserves silhouette and that a turn or fast ascent causes no visible jump. |

**Blender's role.** Build reusable, meter-scale rock, plant and animal families, plus small scenes showing how they meet the seabed. Give each family a scale reference, attachment point, material, animation needs, distance variants and provenance. Export a sample of each through the existing pipeline immediately. Lighting, placement, swimming and frame time are signed off in the native game. This lets the art team spot a broken material or a weightless-looking plant before making dozens of variants. ## Production order and review gates

| Milestone | Deliverable | Ready to move on when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Inventory and baseline | One coverage map, a regional habitat and asset list, a list of source gaps, and baseline native swims and timing traces. Each item has an owner, dependency and status. | Every playable wet tile has a known source or named fallback. Each region has a repeatable route. Existing art is marked for reuse, revision or replacement. |
| 2. Whole-map playable foundation | One build with continuous depths, basic regional surfaces and habitats, recognizable shoreline and working entry and exit. Use simple assets wherever fine detail is pending. | Sweep all wet tiles for missing depth and seams. Swim the routes above and check collision, shoreline alignment and gradual changes between habitats. Resolve data or scale errors here. |
| 3. Three finished reference swims | Produce one native reel with **Mile Rocks** from ledge through kelp into open sand, **Ocean Beach** from the breakers over a sand trough, and **the Ferry Building** from recognizable waterfront down beside pilings and back up. Bring lighting, sound, motion, animals and seabed materials together on each. | The three places are distinguishable in motion with labels hidden. Review matching camera passes, including repeated waterline crossings, and measure the cost of each enabled feature. Record specific failures against the shared system or asset family. |
| 4. Regional rollout | Apply the reviewed rules and libraries across each mapped setting. Author exceptions for surveyed landmarks, distinct formations and habitat edges. Keep an open-water and bare-bottom control route. | All routes, including borders and locations away from bookmarks, pass a swim inspection. Dense kelp, sheltered mud and bare shelf retain their different character. |
| 5. Release review | Package a coverage report, comparable clips, performance results, source credits and open issues. | Meet the frame budget, complete the visual review and record its verdict before changing defaults or making a quality claim. |

World data and water own depths, habitats and optical inputs. Environment art owns the Blender library and reference scenes. Rendering owns light, motion on screen, streaming and profiling. Player and audio own swimming, shore transitions and sound. The app owns reproducible routes and captures. Use one backlog keyed by region and workstream. A defect seen in several regions goes to the shared system's owner before anyone retouches individual sites.

## How to tell when it works

- **Geography.** Save depth-versus-distance profiles along the beach, a channel edge and the offshore transition. Compare them with the input elevations at their native resolution and document discrepancies. Inspect both sides of tile boundaries and the edges of the mapped area.
- **Visuals and movement.** For each reference swim, record shore approach, wave-height eye level, descent, travel along the bed, a look up and resurfacing. Repeat a fixed route with clear and overcast lighting. A stationary submerged camera should show a stable rock while light, silt and fronds move independently. Look for floating plants, synchronized schools, identical distant crowns, tiled caustics and sudden exposure changes.
- **Performance.** At 1920 × 1080 on an idle machine, measure matched feature-on and feature-off runs on the same release build. Record render-thread CPU, GPU and worker times around first entry, repeated crossings, dense habitat and streaming boundaries. The whole-frame median, p95 and worst frame must each stay **under 10 ms on every benchmark path**. Profile any overrun before increasing asset density. [Frame budgets](../native/README.md#performance-target)
- **Review evidence.** Use fresh starts, matched cameras, time, weather and settings. Keep Blender references clearly labeled, and use native footage for the swim review. These water routes supplement the established eight-card blind review and Ferry Building corridor milestone. The recorded AAA verdict is **REJECT** until a new blind round changes it. [Visual quality gate](VISUAL_QUALITY_GATE.md)

The next deliverable is **the coverage map with the three baseline swims and their frame timings**. That gives us a whole-world view of what is missing, what already works and which expensive decisions deserve an early finished example.
